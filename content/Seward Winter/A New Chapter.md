Winter is here in Seward and my life is much different than it was 4.5 months ago. 

Materially my life is much different now than it was in early July and I have just sort of started to settle into life in Seward, for better or for worse. I don't know if I want to stay here for too much longer. The isolation and cultural lacking is getting boring and I feel like I have made enough money and spent enough time away to go appreciate the world more. 

I came to Seward primarily to make money, but also as an adventure, but I have made what I feel like is enough money to leave in February and then return in may or June to start my bike journey, and also feel okay with this leg of my journey. Idk yet though. It would change the trip a lot and I do have an easy and lucrative setup going right now so its tempting to come back and make more so I can be unemployed and bike for longer. However I think its foolish to put money and work over living the life you dream of and I have a dream presenting itself to me and it seems incredibly foolish to lose out on that. 

The dream is traveling with Mutale in Asia for a few months. I love Mutale and I want nothing more than to travel and be with her. This epic opportunity is right in front of me as she will be in Asia and I have the means to join her. I think this is something I have to do. A life experience I would always regret missing. 

I am looking forward to this unexpected development in my life. It reminds me of what uncle Dave at the breeze inn would talk to me about. Chasing women around the globe, following his passions without abashment. Living with lust and passion. I liked Dave despite him being an old man with a drinking problem and a volatile temper. He partied with Hunter S. Thomson in Aspen during the 80s. Did a shit ton of cocaine. He would quite often show up to work hammered drunk or woozily hungover. I couldn't tell sometimes if he was lying or just genuinely had a wild life. Beyond his tall tales of cocaine benders in Aspen he had (allegedly) led a life as a real estate guy or property manager in Orange County. He was married, had a house on the beach, handfulla kids, frequent trips to Europe,... a classically wealthy American life, but he lost it all somehow and left. Stories like this are romantically tragic and its impossible to capture the complexity in a conversation over EDM blasting from my speaker behind the line at a greasy diner in small town Alaska. He said he still had money but he physically can't retire or stop working because he needs the chaos of a busy kitchen. He needs the motion. An addicts character, but really a good guy. 

Too much about Dave. In a way I miss working at the Breeze inn. It was fun on occasion and now my life is so boring. I don't regret it for its already over, but I am remorseful over how I quit because I left without notice and that fucks over the other employees. Regret is a useless feeling. Reflection is good, but one learns and moves on from reflection, regret just lingers and causes grief over what has already been done and cannot be changed. 

Now that I plan on leaving Alaska for a while I have been reflecting overall on my choice to come up here. It was a good choice, but I haven't made the best choices since being here. I think I've been too responsible and have neglected my social needs too much, I don't really go out and socialize ever, and I don't have any real friends here. Do I regret this? I try not to, but it's hard not to imagine how different my life would be if I had put effort into making friends and finding a community here. It would be harder to leave, and thats probably why I never did it. The plan was always to leave, now its just coming sooner than I thought. It's easier to say goodbye to people you don't love intimately and my calculus for this time in my life was take the loneliness in order to not suffer the goodbye. I am not yet convinced that this is the right way to live, but we will see after I leave. 

The process of leaving slo was one of grief and I didn't want to recreate that. I think this is how my parents have lived much of their lives as well. They don't have a large number of friends because they have chosen to move around so much and never put too many roots in a place.
