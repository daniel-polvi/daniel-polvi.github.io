We take a few things to be understood as prerequisites: 
1. contrast is critical for perception of reality
2. ​phenomena are not reality but rather 'images' generated by decoding information emitted by the real world

Number 2 is factual based on our best understanding of neuroscience and how the brain tunes into absolute reality in order to construct our phenomenological reality. I do not understand neuroscience at all on a technical biological level, but this much I do understand. What you see is not totally real. Yes it is real for all our intents and purposes. A flame will burn you, a knife will cut you, but what is a flame and what is a knife? What exactly is blood and why is it red like flames,  inedible berries, and deadly spiders? Why are we primordially afraid of red things? Naturally it seems quite good for the continuation of humanity that in our model, we don't like seeing human blood, or poisonous organisms. But red is only such in our model. My real point here, minus this terrible analogy, is that its just a model. Its not real. But I just said it is real. So which is it? Well okay, it is real, but the way we see it isn't it's absolute and factual essence. So what it is then? What is real? What does reality actually look and feel like? Unfortunately this is unanswerable. Everything we do is biased because we are only physically capable of tuning into a very narrow array of the electro magnetic spectrum, the visible light spectrum. Though we have technology to collect, transmit, and utilize essentially the entire spectrum, we will only ever see contrasting differences between electromagnetic waves with wavelengths between 380 and 750 nanometers. Pretty limited really. Kind of sad to imagine how much else there is to see and feel in the universe we will always be physically barred from. On the brighter side though we have other ways to tune into the world and incorporate many types energy into our constructed world. Sensation is a big one. To feel heat and cold, pleasure and pain, where does this come from? In a similar way to your retina, a clump of nerve cells attuned to the energy the visual spectrum of electromagnetic radiation transmits to it, other types of nerve cells all over your body are attuned to receive different types and amounts of energy whipping around constituting the universe, and send your brain information so it can display a construction of the energy as an imagined physical space that helps us survive. 

Our phenomenal world is not a faithful recollection of absolute universal reality; on top of not being faithful, it fundamentally depends on binary contrast. It could be that we are accurate in the binary model we understand neurons to exhibit, or perhaps that understanding is informed by some learned bias of the human mind towards simplicity. Chicken or egg argument really, but what is definitely true is that our basic level of cognition is binary. On or off. Fire or not. Does the stimulus to the preceding cell in a chain of neurons produce an adequately high electrical current to stimulate the next one to do the same? Everything you have ever experienced and ever will is emergent from some complicated network of on and off switches. To think absolute reality would be so simple is incredibly foolish, however I can accept that the human mind is simple to the point where that is the best we can do. Not to downplay what we have, I personally love it, and find it so amazing that all of this is just emergent behavior from 1s and 0s essentially. 

The question then how does consciousness, and what I'm more concerned with, meaning, arise. Could it be that these things are all just material circumstances emergent from complex interactions of a purely physical system, or could it be that there is something related to consciousness and meaning stored in the ultimately small, truly indivisible amount of energy. Anyone who claims to know for certain is lying or diluted. There is no way to test this, unless we discover some sort of consciousness boson and can some how publicly prove that it related to consciousness; such is the hard problem of consciousness. I think a debate of materialism versus pan consciousness is very interesting, but ultimately unresolvable. It is a problem that in my lifetime at least will never be solved. The very basics of constructing images based on neural activity has started, but even if we could fully project "name's" phenomenal reality, there is no way to prove with absolute certainty that "name" is not a p-zombie. At least though what I can prove to myself is that I am conscious because I feel consciousness, (cogito ergo sum) and my life has meaning because I feel the meaning. These are easily enough to accept premises, I am not saying that these things are the fundamental nature of the universe, I am indeed saying making any sort of definite claim about the fundamental nature of the universe is foolish, but I do think that they at the very least consciousness and meaning exist phenomenologically. 

Because of this existence though, also emptiness, or a lack of meaning, and non-consciousness must also exist in our phenomenological reality. In the same way images created with light cannot be legible without darkness as a canvas, the neurological conditions that project feelings of meaning cannot be felt without the background of emptiness to compare them against.
