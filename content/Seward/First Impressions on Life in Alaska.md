	Written:09-07-2026

Writing this from the bunkhouse on Port ave, Seward, AK, occupied by me, a 70 year woman, and 15 students from Kazakhstan. Things we all have in common: we live here and work at the breeze inn. 
​
​​​​​​​​	Landing in Alaska I had nothing going for me except a phone with 30% charge, a laptop with about the same, and a bike waiting at UPS for me. My headphones died early in the flight and I was unfortunately subject to the grotesque sound of those far too close to me for a period of time. A small fight broke out on board, just a few rows back and across from me. It was morbidly entertaining but genuinely sad. A kid could not stop screaming crying about who knows what, and the mom was detached at first, telling the maybe 6 year old boy: "can you stop! ugh." which soon devolved into yelling at him to "Shut the fuck up". This, as you may imagine, did nothing to sooth the unruly boy. After maybe 15 minutes of crying and screaming by the young boy and equally immature mother a man got up to tell them off. A flight attendant had to separate them and show the man back to his seat. His stomach was impressively developed and he hid his patchy hair with a beret. However, in no time the plane landed and I stepped into the Alaskan air once again. 

​	Fresh air up here, even in the city that is Anchorage. Rode the bus into town, they don't take card or tap to pay or anything, its either $2 cash or a voucher from an app. I only had $1 in my wallet. Blocking traffic the bus driver told me to just get on, but a man behind me covered my fair with his QR code, I offer him the dollar bill, he refused. A bit overwhelmed I find a seat. Looking out the window on the way into downtown I remember why I was adamant about not living in Anchorage. Hopping off the bus into a sad crowd of mostly native alaskans hunched or otherwise lurking around the station. Addicted and impoverished seemed to be the common factor. 

​	At this point I am tired and hungry, but I have a strong desire to get out of downtown anchorage, and generally anchorage ASAP. After picking my bike up from the UPS store I ask the clerk where is a good place to build up my bike. She tells me that just in the parking lot is cool, so I lug my bikebox maybe 100 feet to the common parking lot with the city hall or something like that, some city gov building. There is security in the lot but they never bother me as I string out all my belongings on the hubba of a planter. In fact the only people to bother me are some business casual people from inside who ask me to take a photo for them. Seemed to be someones last day at work. I talk to one lady from the group who welcomes me to Alaska and provides stoke and good wishes for my adventure. She recommended me a park in anchorage I can sleep in, but I opted not to do this. 

​	Maybe a few minutes after this, idk my sense of time was off, a man and woman approach my planter and also post up there. The woman is distressed about how his friend was supposed to pick them up but he's not here and they can't get ahold of him. But the guy is lowkey chillin and ignoring her. She keeps calling him daddy in a whining voice and he keeps walking away from her. I was less than 5 feet away from them assembling my bike, and he was practically sitting on my sleeping bag on this ledge, but neither of them ever acknowledged me or really seemed to notice I was there. Strange. 

​	But finally after maybe an hour of fumbling I get my bike all put together and everything strapped away in their respective bags. My mom had texted me: where are you staying tonight? and I chose not to answer yet because I did not know and needed to focus on getting my bike set up. My chain had broken in transit, or rather became twisted to the point of frustration so I broke it and went on my way to get a new one. My mom texted me again and then called me about where I was staying so I sat down and found a campsite, nearly 30 miles away. I really do hate to make her worry about me. But I carry on to a bike shop, near 5 blocks away, to get a new chain. There are two young guys working there, maybe my age or slightly younger. They also welcome me to Alaska and offer stoke for my trip. Unfortunately I was rather strung out at the time after sleeping not enough the past few days, hitting a mean travel day, and only eating some nuts and a Starbucks breakfast sandwich, so I wasn't too interested in talking. But very kindly they gave me a free tube and let me install my chain in the shop on the stand. The shop was funny, very disorganized and just these two guys running the place. But cheers to Downtown Bicycle Rental, Sales, & Repair in Anchorage. 

​	From downtown its 9 miles to a grocery and 20 after that to a campground, at this point it is also about 7pm AK time I have been up since 4am midwest time, and eaten approximately 500 calories. I was, regardless of my hunger and desire for sleep, very stoked to be on the bike in Alaska. I ride 90 city blocks crossing 90 streets, passing probably 90 people sleeping in bushes or flying signs to a grocery store, stopping in at a Costco food court along the way. Yippie! In the grocery store it is probably 9pm and I feel pressed for time so I buy really shit groceries, not thinking ahead at all, and get back on the bike to fish curve or some balls campground. 

​	20 beautiful but uneventful miles later I get to camp. Its maybe 11pm and the sun is still out; strange. I was tempted to immediately pass out but I had to put out a facebook post in the Seward employment opportunity facebook group, as I plan to get to Seward tomorrow and I need a place to stay and a job. I make a nice post and pass out. 

​	Waking up whenever was nice, and I felt rested and ready to hitchike to Seward. This however did not work. To make a long story short I biked 11 miles to Girdwood, a little resort town about 90 miles up the way from Seward. I stand outside the Girdwood station for maybe 2 hours, get a little pissed and hungry no one has picked me up. I do however have a bike, and have gotten 2 offers going the wrong way or working, and one offer without room for the bike. No dice. I go to the station get a slice of pepperoni pizza, and head back out to try my luck. None. No luck at the station, so I bike a little further down the road. Ah nothing again. So, for some reason unknown to me, I bike a little further. I continue to do this for maybe 9 hours. It gets to a point where I realize nobody is going to pick me up, and I need to take the train. Rats. After I accepted defeat I was just kinda wandering around on the bike hoping to find someplace to mentally recharge, and charge my phone and laptop. A place I did find and good fortune I did strike. 

​	It was a visitor center and train depot. The open sign was glowing, but the door locked. I snoop around for an outside outlet but find nothing, so I sit on a bench and stare at the mountains, working up will to bike back the 9 miles to Girdwood. After a few minutes of lonesome contemplation 3 women pull up in a van and get out. They too see the open sign and check the door, only to be let down and put out. They remark about this or that being closed but the sign is on, I agree and we end up conversing. I ask them where they're going, hoping to score a ride. They're coming from Seward. No worries, I have lost hope of getting there today. They are from Texas, retirees on an Alaskan quest to serve under-serviced communities. As two use the two portapotties, one remains and chats with me. I lament about my struggles hitchhiking and she offer condolences and ask if I am still in contact with my parents. I say 'yeah, I was just texting with my mom', as I was; she seemed relieved. How old are you? 20 I say. :0 she reacts. 20! wow you're really out here. Yupppp. Well lemme give you some food, do you have enough food? I mean yeah I'm okay I was just at the store. But she insisted and gave me some chips and little bites fudge brownies. I profusely thank her and another one of the women, who reminded me of my third grade teacher, Mrs. Gullo asked if we can all say a prayer for young Daniel today. Of course I say yes and we all huddle together arms around each other and she leads a prayer of good luck, and safety for me. I tell them my mom would be so happy to know this happened, and when I told her she was indeed relieved to hear this. 

​	I said goodbye to my Texan friends, walk away to my bench, set down my bottle and go to use the restroom. When I return there is a bologna sandwich sitting under my bottle. The road always provides. 

​	I do find an outlet on the building and charge my shit and eventually pedal back to Girdwood, not before trying again to hitchhike. I eventually come to reason, book a train ticket and pedal back to town. 

​	There are so many more details to this day that I could write and write about like the way time slows down and the almost relaxing drone of the road becomes an unbearable grinding as a double trailer semi rips past at 65, 4 feet from your fleshy vessel, momentarily turning a headwind to stillness, then just as quickly pulling you into a vortex of turbulent air, only to return back to headwind before you can catch your balance again. Or the brief but beautiful conversation I had with a bald woman in a de-commissioned ice cream truck. Any experience from any day can pulverized for its essence and painted on a canvas. I guess which colors, which experiences, we choose to attach meaning to and paint our lives with says much about what we value. On that note the rest of this day was pleasant but not particularly worth writing about in detail. I feel like I should catalog it to some extent tho so I will. This website is basically unlimited paper lol its just a matter of me being to lazy to put everything into decent prose with some coherence. 

​	Yeah so I biked back to town and sat again in the same place I started the day, ate some ramen outside the gas station and pedaled up to the campsite in town. They were setting up for the Girdwood forrest festival which was cool. I talked to Mutale on the phone for like 2.5 hours which was really nice. I can't remember what exactly we talked about, but its always something good, and hearing someones voice I guess is the second best thing to being with them. ​

​	By this point too I had five job offers lined up in Seward. The first from Karl from Seward bike shop, though he was the first, it was only a week long gig and though it paid well it was short term and I didn't want to do it. The second was from the breeze inn, another from millers landing cafe, a third from an inn, and a fourth from a lodge 45 miles outside of Seward. I did decline this one outside of Seward though because I wanted to be in town. I guess spoiler alert from the first paragraph, I took the job at the breeze inn. Ya know I kinda took it on a whim, it was the first place I talked to, and they are like the only place in town that does year round. This is good if I want to stay in Seward for a year, also it seemed chill and it is turning out to be that way. There are many characters there and having worked 5 days so far I am liking it. 

​​	Living with the Kazakhs is quite fun as well. They are very genuinely kind and hospitiable people, it almost makes me ashamed of my American individualism, but to do something kind I bought them/us a bottle of vodka. The night I brought the bottle home we were sitting around bullshitting and the one of 18 years old and scrappy construction said, "should we drink the bottle?"
I said, "Fuck it"
He yells, "YES! LET US FUCK THE BOTTLE!"
So we do just that, 'fuck the bottle'. We don't drink too much, one of the girls keeps trying to hide the bottle from him, and us being all tired turn into bed quite early. However I feel warmly welcome in this dormhouse.
