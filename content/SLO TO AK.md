Slo to Boise to Wisconsin to Seward: 
I guess San Diego in there too. 
​	Oh and I finished college, which felt underwhelming. Honestly a lot of this has felt underwhelming and unceremonious. I never really expected graduation to be a very meaningful event, but I thought at least it would be classy and decent, this however was not really the case. It was rushed and formulaic, just ticking the box. Which did really encapsulate the experience of going to business school at cal poly, actually economics. Unsurprisingly the only inspiring speaker was from the college of liberal arts, she encouraged us to be soft and vulnerable, welcoming to new experience and different peoples. 
​	After the actual ceremony the day was nice, with the family and friends. The week after was crazy tho, moving out physically and mentally. I never realized just how much bullshit was in my apartment until I had to go through every single piece and either keep it or somehow dispose of it. Psychologically too I didn't realize how much bullshit and stress was stored in my mind from the cognitive war of attrition that is college life. There is yet more to unpack there, but a good amount of it was purged in a short time. It was beautiful to leave my longest term home but certainly, and expectedly difficult. 
​	From slo I drove to Boise. The drive was very pleasant actually, I felt free and I enjoyed the vastness of the western United States. The drive was 15 ish hours and I listened to Blood Meridian by Cormac McCarthy for pretty much the entire drive. The book was gruesome and  heavy; so it goes. Personally though, I felt light and easy on the road. I was excited to see a new part of the country and enter a new circumstance. The country out there - Nevada, eastern Oregon, and Idaho - is empty and wide beyond comprehension. I find it strange and twisted how innocent the land seems but how blood ridden it truly is. Blood Meridian made me very aware of the genocidal history of our country; so it goes, I guess. 
​	In Boise I felt out of place. It was hot, stuffy, and conspicuously conservative. The lawns were very well kept, and the facades were high and shiny. No trespassing signs were plentiful, along with 6 - 8 lane stroads and Ram 3500's. I didn't have too much going on while there so I took time to sit around, ride the e-bike, hang with family, and talk to some bums. The bums had good insights on life in Boise: Ya know, if we had this same conversation in like six months we could see the river from here, no leaves, and you'd be wearing 15, NO, 20 pieces of clothes, not three. Im barely wearing three right now. Six months ill be lucky to wear 15. - A tweakers rant on weather patterns in Boise. 
​	I came across these bums in a strange state, my mental and physical beings were wandering in different directions as I was being carried around by the assistance of an electric motor. In the midst of unending sameness and designed curves, I was attempting to make wrong turns to catch a glance of something new. Catch a glance I did of two biker bums. Oh hallelujah I'm a bum. Right off the Eagle river bike path there was a building, perhaps a community center if I remember, some benches a little mosaic, people were coming and going, looking neither happy or sad. But next to this building right off the path was a wooden porch with a concrete bench where these two men were hanging out. Both leaning against a railing, once collapsed now supported by a tree and a few lag screws, facing different directions. One was a large man, facing the sidewalk, dawning a cap, a fishing shirt, and cargo shorts, pockets stuffed. The other was a smaller guy, hunched over the rail facing the river smoking weed from a one hitter pipe disguised as a cigarette. I initially breeze past them on my bike, expecting everyone in Boise to be unwilling to interestingly converse, but suddenly realize this is everything I have ever wanted in this moment. I stop my bike and turn around. I asked them if they are out living on the bike and the man facing me replied: What?! I said bikepacking are you guys into biking, he said yeah. I say hey yeah me too I like to bike, hey reckon you can tell me how to get downtown, the bike path is closed today and the roads here suckkkkkkkk. He was telling me, hey man just ride on the sidewalk you can do that here. I was like oh good to know, these roads have been killing me. We go on to talk about how he was hit by a ford f-250 because a mans dog had jumped on his lap and he lost control of the car and ran him over. He was struck by the car while stopped on his bike and flew and slid on asphalt for 6 feet. His left foot was shattered; so it goes, i suppose. The insurance paid for a new bike and his medical bills, but because he can't pedal the same anymore he converted his surly Ogre (the same bike I ride) into an e-bike, pretty cool.  
​	This led into gripes about large vehicles and the general oversized everything in Boise. Large roads, big facades on buildings, and general big dickery that is emblematic of certain sects of American culture. The other man hobbles a few feet to me and holds out his hand with pipe and lighter, I say whats this? He mumbles: weed. I say no thanks, I can't do all that, but ill take a cig if you got smoke. He obliged and I smoke a cigarette. The larger man tells me more about the history of Boise. Why is it called Boise anyway? LES BOIS! the other man interjected, starting to warm up to my presence. Well the french people came here and saw a lot of trees, les bois means forrest or the trees in french, hense Boise. And do you know the name of the valley were in? I actually did know this one, treasure valley were in. Yes, and why do you suppose that is? Perhaps it was supposed there is treasure here. Yeah they thought there was gold but alas none was really found. We go on to talk about life and the absurdity, but after a while the smoking man gets fed up and keeps repeating the same thing about how we go from bike path to atomic bombs and it could just be so simple if the bike path was open. Ya know if the path was clear it would be so easy to get downtown and we would have never had to even think about, much less talk about atomic bombs and big cars and bogus basin. I was planning to leave anyway but then Matt called me so I rode off to meet him for dinner. 
​	After a nice dinner with the bro I went further into town and wandered around a bit more, it was nice but still pretty normal. 
​	The next day Matt and I went up into the forrest to go shooting and it was really fun, also I like talking to Matt about whatever. I feel like there isn't as much to report here that is remarkable expect for maybe the insanity of shooting guns. When moving out of the apartment Theo and I were joking about just having a rage room party and giving everyone that comes in a baseball bat or golf club and just saying everything must go. Of course we didn't do that, but it does remind me of at the piercing shop in slo getting Theo's ears pierced the guy told us how in a drug induced frenzy him and his friends smashed up an entire room at the maddona inn and this chick with her name on the card that paid for the room had to go to court because the damage was in the 10s of thousands of dollars. In the morning they woke up to banging on the door and they hopped out a broken side window and ran away. Allegedly someone else had started it. But because right before they had gotten into a car crash that also allegedly wasn't their fault they were pissed off. The point of this is, in the same way destroying things feels good in the moment but makes you want more destruction, shooting guns is powerful and cathartic, but also makes you crave that feeling of power and destruction. Getting high in non substance induced ways does interest me tho. Be it climbing mountains or hitchiking or working out. I reckon its a better way to escape the pain of existence than drugs. 
​​	But back to biography: The day before the bum interaction and my journey to find mysterious questions with no answers I went over to my cousins house for dinner. At first I was reluctant to go for I had never really met them as a conscious being, and I tend to get nervous about these things. But being brave I went over there for some tacos. It was a great time me and Steven talked for a long time about traveling and life, plus their little kid was a joy and inspiringly intelligent. Steven was strongly encouraging me to just travel travel travel and be open and vulnerable to new experiences - like the only resonating commencement speaker - and to say yes to things that will make you uncomfortable. I always finding it reassuring to hear from adults I respect that my decision to travel is a good one. 
​​	Next I flew to Chicago and met Collin at the airport where his mom picked us up. I remember at the pickup zone the guys directing traffic were having a terrible time and were just stressed the fuck out. Like how could it possibly be that serious? It's a busy place tho and their whole job is to just tell people not to do stupid shit all day, I guess it makes sense to be stressed, but just be above that. Similar to the restaurant life, its chaotic and people are yelling at you if you fuck up, but its legitimately not that deep. There is nothing on the line besides someone getting their bacon cheeseburger 10 minutes late, or there is a big pile of dishes you need to do. Just be calm and don't let the stress kill you. 
​	Madison was a vibe though, super chill summer time activities, beers, boat, couch. Midwest version of surf, skate, taco. Type. I remember just laughing pretty much the entire time I was there, being with the bros and just big fuckin chillin. Also went to Chicago and flicked the bean, pretty cool city, I would like to go back there. Only there for a few days though and it was fun more than anything. An interesting thing we did was tour the epic campus, the company John works for. The campus was huge and strange. Every building was decorated thematically and being there felt like a fantasy land where in every bathroom there is the ten epic commandments and in every breakroom there is a fridge full of chocolate milk and apple juice. Pure nerd bait. I just tried really hard not to overcritize everything there because it is something I could get too worked up about. 
​	The biggest takeaway from my midwest time is that: we are fundamentally seekers;  regardless of object or reason we seek. 
​	From Chicago I flew to Anchorage, still no job or place to land when I got there.
