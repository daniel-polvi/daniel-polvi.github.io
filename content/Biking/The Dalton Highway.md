[The Dalton highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_Highway)is the road from Prudhoe Bay to Fairbanks, a very remote and gnarly ice road, starting (ending) at the Arctic Ocean, crossing the north slope, passing over the brooks range, and rolling into Fairbanks.  The logistics on this section of road are potentially the trickiest in the world, due to its remoteness and complete lack of services. Prudoe Bay is a serious contender for the end of the world, maybe second only to Utqiagvik (farther north, but off the road system) this makes it a great and totally reasonable place to start my bike trip across the world!

There is a narrow weather window to bike the road and not be wrecked in one way or another. I see the main considerations to be: weather, road condition, and wildlife patterns (bear, moose, and mosquitos). Starting too early could mean freezing temperatures and the risk of late winter storms, but starting too late means temperatures could be unbearably hot in the interior, and being eaten alive by [moosequitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culiseta_alaskaensis)  (Massive mosquitos that form aggressive clouds known to cause despair and outdoor regret, also the unofficial state bird of Alaska!). Also bear and moose can be cranky, hungry, and unpredictable in the start of summer time, as they are just waking up from hibernation after a long winter in one of the harshest places on earth. Also road traffic is a consideration. I have read that the truckers on the road are generally very friendly to bikers and give lots of space, but frankly it seems stressful to have a constant line of semis hauling oil drilling rigs passing me. But I don't want to be totally deserted out there so if, god forbid, if I'm in distress on the side of the road, someone will see me and stop. The point here is I need to figure out the balance between all these extremes and find a compromise. 

Also some of my friends have expressed interest in biking this section with me, because it is super epic, so if they are serious about that I need to plan around that too. I will also have to mail supplies up there beforehand, because there is nowhere to purchase food, and the 414 mile trip can take up to 2 weeks because of slow movement over the ice road. Also getting up there is a trick init of itself. Spoiler alert: not many people want to go up there for tourist reasons, so there isn't much tourist infrastructure for people like myself. But I am sure it is doable, probably just expensive :0. 

There are many things to consider with this section but I also imagine it will be one of the greatest roads in the world to bike on purely out of epic gnarly-ness. I legitimately can't think of a better way to describe the way this road appeals to me. Epic and gnarly are overused words, and I am personally very guilty of this, but in their deep sense biking this road would be both epic and gnarly. Maybe some other overused words: transcendental, wonderful, awesome, breathtaking, radical... The list could go on. spiritual, ridiculous, unbelievable, absurd... maybe it's the variety and extremity of emotions attracts me to doing this road, and really the whole ride. One day on the bike in the deep Alaskan wilderness is more life, more awe, more pain, more danger, more satisfaction, more connection, more everything than one can experience in weeks, months, or even years of a standard working life. So I can't even begin to imagine the life I will experience during 2 years on the bike; it's harmfully overwhelming to even try to imagine it all. To speculate and set expectations will only cause strife and spoil serendipity. 

Wait. The point of this page is the Dalton highway. Getting side tracked, but do I really care enough to give a history lesson here? I could write about why and how the road was built, as I think it is very interesting and I have probably spent longer obsessing about this road than I'd like to admit, but I also feel like it is adequately documented already and I have nothing original to add as I have not been there yet. So all you get for this page is me saying a lot of disconnected and grand adjectives, better luck next time.
