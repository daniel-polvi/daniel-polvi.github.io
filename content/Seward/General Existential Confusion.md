Frank, my co-worker at the breeze, died on the night of his 42nd birthday, 20th of July, 2026. Found dead in his bathtub the next morning surrounded by bottles. He was an alcoholic and we all knew that but he wasn't obviously suicidal, and it just made no sense and felt so tragic and wrong that he died in the way he did. The previous few days he had been drinking at work. I would come in at 6am, same time as him and catch him stumbling up the few stairs to the back door. You could smell it on his breath too, Vodka that is. Its strange to see someone so wasted so early in the morning. During work too he would be obviously out of it, and the other cooks would shouts for him to do something, and he just wouldn't really respond or always be fumbling and just zoned. I wonder what was on his mind in those moments, either far too much or just the blur of his current state. He would also often slip off the line to the back corner where his bag was, coming back with an ever stronger scent of liquor on his breath. 

On the day of his birthday he decided to leave work early, as he felt he didn't want to work on his birthday, he had also just been fucked up and fucking up all week and was obviously tired. I guess that was his last day alive. Such a miserable last day on earth. Overcast, windy, rainy, working at a bullshit restaurant covered in grease, sweat, and pancake batter. Utterly meaningless it must have felt for him, and maybe for good reason. In many ways he had let himself go and couldn't get out of his addiction, but still talked about the future in excited ways, which made it harder to understand his death. His soul wasn't ripe yet. I think people can be ripe to die, even if they personally don't know it. In their life they have exhausted their potential, or rather satisfied their role in the cosmic comedy (or tragedy if you prefer). I believe that Frank had more life to live. 

I have never experienced death in such a way before. All my grandparents died before I knew what was going on. Our dog, Julie, died when I was in 11th grade, but you expect that and she was ready to go. Her soul was mature and it was natural for her to move on. Other than that I only understand death in an abstract way. I used to like to think that I had accepted death. I had this quote on my wall for years and would read it almost every day, 
​
"To fear death, gentlemen, is no other than to think one wise when one is not, to think one knows what one does not know. No one knows whether death may be the greatest of all blessings for a man, yet he fears it as if it is the greatest of evils." 
-Socrates, Plato's apology

Maybe I have accepted only my own death, I certainly don't want to die, in fact, I do a lot of things to make sure I don't die, but I do understand it's not really my problem when I do die. I don't think anything really happens that we can experience in any way similar to our living phenomena, or is explainable using languages with vocabulary developed entirely by living people to describe subjects of life. It is also true that any and all attempts at explaining phenomena after death are purely speculative and cannot be critically examined. However I will never be one to discredit purely speculative endeavors, and do not see why criticality is necessary for such spiritual questions. 

The mystery of randomness is fascinating to me in many ways. The mystery of Faith as they used to say at mass. The day I heard about Franks death was two days later, and was my Friday.
