Should I call this a disclaimer or a philosophical exposé. Probably the latter. 

I am not a documentarian. I do not record the objective truth at all costs. I have strong opinions and often prefer not to write anything down. I seriously dislike biographical writing or factual storytelling. It is so tedious and trifling to write down what I already know happened. 

I do it for you though. You being you, and future me. You who was not there, and me who was, but will forget. You who is curious and me who is reminiscent. I do love to tell stories, in-fact I consider myself a storyteller, but I just sometimes can't stand the legwork of recording facts. 

This comes from two idealogical places, one being socrates and his vow to never write anything down, another being a dislike of clutter, attachment, and hoarding of information. In 11th grade I was introduced to the works of Plato, and through him the ideas of socrates. For some reason I was very attracted to and became attached to Socrates' idea that writing makes mental faculties weak. My connection to and strong conviction of this idea lasted between one or two years. I was philosophically against having a calendar, taking notes, or writing down reminders. I WILL REMEMBER!! I told myself. 

This didn't last forever, shown clearly by the fact you are reading what I wrote down. My life got to a point where it was too complicated to remember it all, and remembering all these facts was taking up mental effort that can be offloaded to paper or digital memory. In an attempt to expand and keep my brain healthy I was in effect making it smaller. Smaller by not allowing technology to be used as annexes and extensions of my mind. In this sense I have come around to collecting information but I still get annoyed with clutter and my tendency to hoard. 

In the same way that trying to take a photo removes you from the very moment you want to capture. Always thinking trying to remember life for later so you can write it down can take away from the experience of just feeling life. The absolute presence and immersion I felt at times when not writing anything and purely existing in my mind was interesting, but fleeting as all things are.
