​	I work at the Breeze inn restaurant and lounge. It's an interesting place. The food is normal, and the people are strange. There too all the people I live with work; them as housekeepers, I as a cook. I also do dishes. Today Sandra, an older woman from Colombia told me, "Haces más paradas que un autobús." meaning I have more stops than a bus. She actually typed it out on google translate and showed it to me. At first I didn't understand, it seemed kind of offensive, and having just looked it up, it is used for someone who is slow or makes a lot of stops in a story, but she was trying to tell me I do every station in the kitchen. It was nice. She is a really lovely woman, very hard worker too. She runs around doing the living shit out of the dishes. Sandra teaches me Spanish phrases as I told her a few days ago, "yo quiero apprender espanol". 

​	Everyone there is a character tho, as is typical with the restaurant life, but a few stand out to me. One is Frankie, a commercial real estate broker turned line cook. Frankie and I talk about econometrics and GIS, how capitalism is stupid, and how he was making 250k in real estate but hated it, and now he makes shit-all at the breeze, but is much happier for it. He doesn't always seem the happiest though, I suppose you much have a crisis of sorts to come to Alaska from corporate in New York. Demons as much as balls to be fair. To just pick up your life, get a truck and trailer and drive across the continent for 7 months from New York to Alaska, is a brave and commendable thing to do. 

​	Frank worked and lived for a few months at the same place I lived and worked at two years ago, the Nauti Otter yurt village. He and I remember our times there fondly. He told me yesterday how someone else there had acquired mushrooms and prescribed him a dose, he "Had never done hard drugs before" but recounted a lovely time he had wandering through the rainforest to end up at a glowing tree below which he sat and observed. He spent the whole night under this tree soaking in its natural majesty, much to the concern of his friends at the village. I wouldn't consider psilocybin too hard of drug. 

​	But speaking of hard drugs, there was a guy passed out on heroin on the bathroom floor today. Unfortunately his name is also Dan. Well Danny, but still cmon. Also known as 'Night Hawk' according to Pineapple, a line cook and our resident Hawaiian. Pineapple used to party with him when he first came to Seward yay nine years ago. Danny went by Night Hawk and would often take his dick out during beer pong games to distract the opponent. Pineapple would chuck the pingpong ball at it. A few days ago, when I first heard of Danny, I had asked Travis, another line cook with some killer neck tats, where Jordan had went. Travis told me he went down to Safeway to steal a bike back again. I was like again?? For some reason people here don't lock up bikes even though there is a known bike theft problem in town. But Danny our resident tweaker had stolen this chicks bike for the second time and it had been spotted locked outside of Safeway. So they sent squad over there to steal it back. This was maybe two hours after Travis had recounted the number of times he had been stabbed and shot. Stabbed twice, shot once. ​

​	Things like these are so strange and extraordinary to me, but just regular life for them. There is nothing that deep about this, simply we come from tremendously different backgrounds, but consequently I just have very little to relate with a lot of the people that work there. At least the American people. 

​	There are a lot of J1's as we call them. The J1 visa is for students from other countries to come to the U.S. and work and exchange culture. There are a lot of them in Seward. Everyone I live with is on the J1 visa, and maybe 10 more people at the restaurant are. There are 4 or 5 guys from Bulgaria, A guy and a girl from Dominican Republic, and a girl from Colombia. A few more I haven't met either. Talking with them is refreshing as they are all students and my age.
