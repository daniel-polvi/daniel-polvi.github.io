Today one of my coworkers asked me, "Dan, why do you look so sad all the time?". I have been asked this question several times in my life, and it always makes me pause. 

The first time I remember it came from a friend during a bout of depression. During a car conversation where one friend was asking me how I am doing and while we spoke, my other friend interjected with this question of 'why are you always so sad, like whats wrong with you'(or something to that effect). The way he asked it was aggressive. Almost to say why can you just get over yourself. In that moment I just blew it off as the stupid comment it was, but I couldn't stop thinking about it after the fact. During this time I was stuck in the past, ruminating over mistakes I had made, a romantic relationship that had fallen through, an unhealthy and short lived one in its wake, and a new one starting I was unsure about. Among, of course, all the other coming of age things that made 19yr old me worry. 

I see now my suffering came from the inability to accept ambiguity as a fact of life. It was unclear to me why and how, me and her, once in love, were no longer able to speak. It was unclear to me how I let myself ignore so many obvious warnings about someone, trust them anyway, and be surprised when I am hurt. I was dealing with the biggest emotional loss of my life up to then and the mystery of it consumed me. There were no clear reasons why, but I only came to know this after being stuck in my mind for months, analyzing the past trying to reason through life. Why did we stop feeling for each other. Why did it have to end so terribly. I didn't know then and I don't know now. However I do know now that I can't know for there is nothing more to know expect for that it happened, it sucked, and now its okay. 

The next time came from a total stranger, which was as startling as it was comforting. This was roughly 9 months after the first time I was asked. I was a different person by then. I had seen more, learned more, spoken to more people, and felt a wider array of feelings. It happened on a summer evening in southern Italy. Sitting at an crumbling concrete amphitheater overlooking the sea, cycling physically between rolling and chain-smoking cigarettes, and poorly playing the blues on my harmonica, and emotionally between self pity and grandeur. I was a sad scene, stereotypically lost in the existential sauce. Having felt isolated in so many regards, by language, culture, physical distance, and emotional connection, I suppose my ravenous appetite for human connection emanated from my very being.

I was lost somewhere in my mind where sobs of absurd laughter rang loud when I was interrupted by a hello. An english word spoken by a young British man. I think 17 he was, with his group of family friends who were all on vacation. First he said hello, then when I responded in English he just asked me if I'm okay. I was startled and embarrassed so I just auto replied oh I'm fine, but he told me he can tell I wasn't. I scoffed as to move it forward and he asked me if I can roll, I obliged and rolled a large joint. We proceed to smoke and listen to trap music from his iphone.

While high we have a meaningless conversation about the United Kingdom and his Highschool bla bla and how omg our parents are gonna know we smoked when we're at dinner. After a little bit they left, not before getting my instagram. However nice it was to converse with these young British lads, I felt more comfortable once they were gone and I was alone again. Back in my self absorbed moment of despair I start to reflect on why I am feeling so sorry for myself. Being alone is no reason to be unhappy. Sure a shared joy is a double joy, but a lonesome joy is a joy nonetheless. Loneliness is wonderfully painful and ultimately necessary if you want to know yourself. Feeling so very alone and far from everyone and everything you know and love is the only way to determine what you alone want for your life. 

In a similar vein is boredom. Boredoms not a burden anyone should bear. Unless you want to appreciate joy and entertainment fully. Ascetic deprivation of entertainment and pleasure. Yin and Yang. Li and Shih. What is pleasure without pain. What is joy without sorrow. What is entertainment without boredom. These days we are so constantly entertained, boredom is becoming a choice. A hard choice to make: to be brave enough to withstand the sole company of your own thoughts. My life right now is incredibly boring, I think this is why I look "sad" all the time, according to Aly at least. I don't really do exciting things very much and much of what makes me smile are simple pleasures like engaging conversation and the satisfaction of clean sheets and a vacuumed carpet. I had been running on the mountains but that became unsustainable as I was doing far too much and hurting my body. Boy was it exciting though. Talk about a good high, run straight up a 3000 foot mountain in 2 miles, then straight back down. Engaging too. I never did come face to face with a bear, but with a moose I did have a stare down, in which I promptly surrendered. Humbling it was.

But boredom! Boredom is pleasurable if you enjoy the length of life. It is torture if you rush to the next. Boredom should never be equivocated with unhappiness, and further, boredom will not cause or even suggest unhappiness to those at peace with themselves.

In todays world where entertainment has become the default boredom has become a choice you can easily go the rest of your entire life without feeling real true absolutely deprived boredom. This is a shame as boredom is a beautifully painful experience. Like all forms of pain, boredom brings us sharply into the moment. I recently have been examining my reactions to boredom to get a better idea of how I can find peace in my mind. Most often I quell my boredom with some sort of consciousness shifting behavior. In a particular, but not strictly descending order, actions like doing drugs, scrolling, games, music, podcasts, videos, exercise, socializing, or shopping are how I personally shift my consciousness to stop boredom. Often I don't face my boredom with courage. In this sense I am a coward. I am afraid or otherwise unwilling to face the vague but excruciating pain boredom mixed with an overactive mind brings. 

Boredom being uncomfortable seems to be adaptive though. Evolutionary I'm saying. First of all, I am in no way qualified to make claims about the evolutionary value of certain emotions or conscious affects, but it does seem that a natural conviction to always do something rather than nothing would be adaptive. On a caveman scale, in a world where basic needs still required constant effort to be met I figure that this unwillingness to do nothing would help ones genes propagate. On a modern and personal level, my hatred and unwillingness or maybe inability to be bored has led me to doing a lot of fun and thought provoking things. Things like inspiring creation, fun travel, and generally just living a full life. This trait though is also why I have struggled with substance misuse, and is what attention retention algorithms use to steal your life from you for profit. I assume this dichotomous outcome is more prevalent in, but not necessarily unique to, the modern world where exploitative entertainment is the default and boredom is never mandatory. 

A good reason to embrace boredom is to save yourself from addiction and exploitation. Or another way around this is finding healthier and more legitimate ways to be get high or expound boredom. Practically this looks like spending less time at the bar and more time at the gym. Or spending less time scrolling and more time climbing mountains. Sure it's easier said than done, but really what else is there to do? Assuming you're like everyone else and only have this one life, one very short and fragile life at that, how dare you take it for granted? How dare you waste it consumed by grief or abuse? I say just get up and do it damn it. Alternative Gen-z phrasing: fuck it we ball.
